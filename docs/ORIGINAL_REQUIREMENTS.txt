Act as a principal enterprise software architect, procurement operations specialist, and senior full-stack engineer.

Build a production-oriented procurement and inventory management platform for multinational corporations. Deliver a working application with a database, backend, frontend, vendor portal, tests, deployment configuration, and documentation—not just mockups or a product specification.

The platform must answer:
- What do we need to purchase?
- Which vendors supply each product or service, in each country?
- Who was invited to quote, and who responded?
- Which offer provides the best overall value?
- Who needs to approve the purchase?
- What was ordered, received, invoiced, and paid?
- What inventory is available, reserved, or running low?
- Which suppliers consistently perform well?

Use the following requirements as the product specification.

## 1. Architecture and implementation

Default stack:
- Next.js and TypeScript for the application.
- PostgreSQL with Prisma for persistent data.
- A durable background job queue for invitations, notifications, imports, and scheduled tasks.
- S3-compatible storage for documents and attachments.
- Docker Compose for local development.

You may adjust the stack if the existing repository requires it. Explain significant changes.

Create a modular monolith with clear boundaries between procurement, vendors, inventory, finance, approvals, and reporting. Avoid unnecessary microservices.

Use real database-backed workflows. Do not simulate successful actions. If an external integration is unconfigured, clearly display that status and provide a development adapter.

Before implementation, inspect the repository and its instructions. Then write a concise implementation plan and proceed autonomously with reasonable assumptions. Ask only about decisions that materially block implementation.

## 2. Corporate structure and access

Support:
- Multiple independent corporate tenants.
- Multiple legal entities within each tenant.
- Countries, offices, departments, cost centers, warehouses, and delivery locations.
- Entity-specific base currencies, purchasing policies, tax settings, and approval thresholds.
- Consolidated group reporting without mixing legal entity accounting records.
- English and Arabic, including right-to-left layouts.
- Local time zones and date formats.

Roles:
- Group administrator.
- Entity administrator.
- Procurement manager and buyer.
- Department requester.
- Budget owner and approver.
- Warehouse manager and receiving staff.
- Finance and accounts payable.
- Auditor with read-only access.
- Vendor administrator and vendor employee.

Enforce permissions on the backend and at the record level. Vendor users can access only their own invitations, submissions, orders, and documents. Prevent cross-tenant and unauthorized cross-entity access.

## 3. Vendor onboarding and supplier directory

Build a vendor portal and internal vendor management area.

Procurement staff must be able to:
- Invite vendors through email.
- Track invitation delivery, acceptance, expiry, and onboarding progress.
- Review and approve vendor applications.
- Suspend or block vendors with recorded reasons.
- Manage multiple contacts and vendor users.

Capture:
- Legal and trading names.
- Registration and tax identifiers.
- Countries served and delivery areas.
- Addresses, contacts, and branches.
- Supported currencies and payment terms.
- Product categories, brands, products, and services supplied.
- Minimum order quantities, lead times, and delivery charges.
- Certificates, insurance, and supporting documents with expiry dates.
- Bank details with restricted access and an approval process for changes.
- Contracts, agreed price lists, and purchasing history.
- Qualification status and performance scorecards.

Provide filters such as:
“Show approved suppliers of printer consumables that deliver to Cairo within three days.”

Maintain an explicit supplier-to-item relationship containing the vendor SKU, manufacturer reference, unit of measure, pack size, price history, lead time, MOQ, region, contract reference, and last verified date.

Keep vendor claims separate from internally verified information. Flag potential duplicates for review rather than merging automatically.

## 4. Product and service master

Maintain:
- Internal SKU, name, description, category, and manufacturer.
- Goods versus services.
- Specifications and attachments.
- Base units, purchasing units, and explicit pack conversions.
- Approved substitutes and equivalent items.
- Preferred and alternate suppliers.
- Stock tracking settings.
- Reorder points, safety stock, and target stock.
- Optional batch, serial number, and expiry tracking.
- Entity-specific tax codes.

Prevent invalid unit conversions. Preserve conversion factors used in historical transactions even if the master data changes later.

## 5. Purchase requisitions and budgets

Employees must be able to create requisitions with:
- Items or services, quantities, specifications, and required dates.
- Delivery location, department, cost center, and budget.
- Business justification, urgency, and attachments.
- Suggested suppliers and substitutes.

Support draft, submitted, returned, approved, rejected, cancelled, and fulfilled states.

Check available inventory before recommending a purchase. Suggest internal stock transfers where appropriate.

Track budgets as allocations, commitments, and actual expenditure. Avoid double-counting when requisitions become purchase orders and invoices.

Support configurable rules for over-budget requests, emergency purchases, and single-source procurement.

## 6. RFQs and vendor invitations

Buyers must be able to create an RFQ from approved requisitions or directly with authorization.

RFQs must include:
- Unique reference and version.
- Legal entity and purchasing contact.
- Item specifications, quantities, and units.
- Delivery requirements and locations.
- Submission deadline with time zone.
- Commercial terms and required documents.
- Evaluation criteria and weights.
- Invited vendors and clarification history.

Support:
- Invitations to selected vendors.
- Secure authenticated vendor submissions.
- Reminders and deadline extensions.
- RFQ amendments with notifications and acknowledgement.
- Clarifications shared with all relevant participants where appropriate.
- Draft and submitted quotations.
- Revised offers with full version history.
- Configurable sealed bidding: evaluators cannot view offers before opening.
- Explicit handling of late offers.

A submitted quotation must be preserved as a versioned snapshot. Vendors cannot silently alter an offer already under evaluation.

## 7. Receive and normalize offers

Vendors must be able to submit:
- Line-item prices.
- Currency and applicable taxes.
- Discounts.
- Freight and other charges.
- MOQ and offered pack sizes.
- Lead time and delivery schedule.
- Payment terms and quotation validity.
- Availability and partial supply.
- Alternatives and technical deviations.
- Warranty and supporting attachments.

Allow internal users to enter offers received outside the portal, recording their source and attaching the original document.

Support CSV and Excel import with validation and a review screen before saving.

Optional AI-assisted PDF extraction must:
- Show extracted values and source references.
- Flag uncertainty.
- Require human confirmation.
- Never invent missing prices, quantities, or terms.

## 8. Quotation comparison and supplier selection

Build a side-by-side comparison matrix showing:
- Vendors as columns and requested lines as rows.
- Unit prices normalized to the requested unit.
- Offered quantity, pack size, and MOQ.
- Discounts, freight, taxes, and other charges.
- Delivery time, availability, and validity.
- Payment terms.
- Technical compliance and deviations.
- Missing or noncompliant lines.
- Historical prices and supplier performance.

Calculate both payable total and evaluation cost. Handle recoverable versus nonrecoverable taxes according to configurable entity rules.

For currency comparison:
- Use a recorded exchange rate, source, and timestamp.
- Preserve the original offer currency.
- Snapshot the rate used for each evaluation.
- Clearly distinguish estimates from final accounting values.

Make calculation formulas visible and exportable. Do not assume missing charges are zero.

Support:
- Lowest compliant price.
- Weighted evaluation across price, quality, lead time, payment terms, and supplier performance.
- Mandatory eligibility requirements before weighted scoring.
- Configurable and documented score normalization.
- Whole-RFQ awards and split awards by line or quantity.
- Capacity, MOQ, and delivery constraints.
- Recorded reasons for selecting a more expensive supplier.

Recommendations must be explainable. AI may summarize tradeoffs but cannot approve purchases or override eligibility rules.

## 9. Approvals and auditability

Build configurable approval workflows based on:
- Legal entity.
- Department and cost center.
- Purchase amount and currency.
- Category.
- Budget availability.
- Emergency or single-source status.
- Vendor risk.

Support sequential and parallel approvals, delegation, escalation, rejection, and requests for changes.

Enforce separation of duties and configurable restrictions on self-approval.

Material changes to an approved transaction must trigger reapproval. Preserve the approval rules and transaction version used for each decision.

Record an append-only audit trail showing actor, timestamp, action, reason, and relevant before-and-after values. Ordinary administrators must not be able to edit audit history.

## 10. Purchase orders and supplier fulfilment

Generate purchase orders from approved awards.

Include:
- Buyer and supplier legal details.
- Delivery and billing addresses.
- Items, quantities, units, prices, taxes, and currency.
- Delivery schedule and payment terms.
- Contract reference and attachments.
- Approval history and document version.

Support:
- Supplier acknowledgement.
- Partial acceptance.
- Partial deliveries.
- Backorders.
- Amendments with versioning and approval.
- Cancellations.
- Returns and credit references.
- Outstanding quantity and overdue tracking.

Generate professional downloadable PDF purchase orders.

## 11. Receiving and inventory

Implement an inventory transaction ledger, not an editable stock counter.

Support:
- Goods receipts linked to purchase order lines.
- Partial receipts.
- Inspection, acceptance, rejection, and quarantine.
- Warehouse and bin locations.
- Reservations and releases.
- Departmental stock issues.
- Warehouse transfers with in-transit status.
- Returns.
- Stock counts and approved adjustments.
- Optional batches, serial numbers, and expiry dates.

Show:
- On-hand stock.
- Reserved stock.
- Available stock.
- Quarantined stock.
- In-transit stock.
- Incoming purchase order quantities.

Prevent duplicate posting and enforce configurable negative-stock rules.

Provide reorder recommendations based on consumption, safety stock, lead time, and outstanding orders. Identify eligible suppliers for each recommendation.

Support a documented inventory valuation method and prevent silent retrospective changes to posted transaction costs.

## 12. Invoices and finance

Support vendor invoice capture and:
- Duplicate invoice detection.
- Two-way matching for applicable services.
- Three-way matching between purchase orders, accepted receipts, and invoices.
- Configurable price and quantity tolerances.
- Exception queues.
- Credit notes.
- Due dates and payment status.
- Finance exports.

Payments must be recorded or synced from an authorized finance integration. Do not claim a payment has occurred merely because an invoice was approved.

Keep this platform’s purchasing and matching scope explicit. Do not pretend it replaces a full general ledger.

## 13. Contracts and supplier performance

Track:
- Contract validity and renewal reminders.
- Approved pricing and volume tiers.
- Entity and category coverage.
- Contract utilization.
- Off-contract purchases.
- On-time delivery.
- Fill rate.
- Quality rejections.
- Response time.
- Price variance.
- Disputes and resolution history.

Display metric definitions and supporting transactions.

## 14. Dashboards and reporting

Create dashboards for:
- Procurement workload.
- Open requisitions and pending approvals.
- RFQ response rates.
- Upcoming deadlines.
- Spend by entity, department, category, and vendor.
- Budget utilization.
- Purchase price variance.
- Overdue orders.
- Inventory shortages and excess stock.
- Supplier performance.
- Invoice matching exceptions.
- Contract and document expiry.

Clearly define savings baselines and avoid unsupported savings claims.

Provide searchable tables, filters, drill-downs, and CSV/Excel exports.

## 15. Integrations and automation

Create documented API and webhook boundaries for ERP, accounting, identity, email, and catalog systems.

Provide:
- Retryable background jobs.
- Idempotency keys.
- Delivery and failure logs.
- Secure webhook verification.
- Notifications with links to the relevant records.
- Configurable scheduled reminders.

Design adapter interfaces for Odoo, SAP, Microsoft Dynamics, and Oracle. Implement only integrations that can be tested with available credentials or a documented sandbox. Label all other adapters as unimplemented.

Use a local email inbox for development. Do not send real invitations without explicit deployment configuration.

## 16. Security and reliability

Include:
- Strong authentication and production SSO integration boundaries.
- Secure sessions.
- Tenant and entity isolation.
- Server-side authorization.
- Encryption in transit and appropriate protection for sensitive data.
- Secret management outside source code.
- Secure uploads with size/type limits and malware-scanning integration.
- Expiring document download links.
- Rate limiting.
- Backups and documented restore procedures.
- Structured logs and monitoring.
- Configurable retention and deletion policies.

Do not claim universal legal or regulatory compliance. Identify requirements that need country-specific review.

Use decimal-safe money calculations and database transactions for inventory posting, awards, approvals, and financial commitments.

## 18. Build order and demonstration

Implement a complete core workflow first:
Requisition → approval → RFQ → vendor invitation → quotation submission → comparison → award approval → purchase order → partial receipt → inventory update → invoice matching.

Then add the remaining modules in coherent increments.

Seed clearly fictional demonstration data with:
- One multinational tenant with at least three legal entities.
- Multiple currencies, departments, and warehouses.
- A second tenant for isolation testing.
- At least eight vendors and thirty products/services.
- Different pack sizes, currencies, freight terms, and lead times.
- A split award, partial delivery, rejected receipt, and invoice exception.

Create a reproducible demonstration showing why the cheapest unit price may not be the lowest compliant delivered cost.
